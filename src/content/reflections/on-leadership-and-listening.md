I'm reflective by nature. I want to understand how systems fit together before going into details. This includes trying to understand why people act in a certain way. If I know what is driving actions of others, it is much easier to find common ground and motivate people towards a common goal. I've had various leadership roles in the past, and I wonder if it's my interest in learning about peoples motivation that has led to these roles. 

As part of a leadership program at AstraZeneca we were asked to draft a leadership statement. I found the exercise to be very useful. I do not follow it perfectly, but I revisit it at times whenever I need direction. 
This is what I wrote: 

**Leadership Statement**

_"I belive in the intrinsic value of respect and fairness. I act on data, apply systems-thinking and coaching to navigate the complexities of decision-making to guide my team. I'm committed to fostering empowerment, I'm advocating for my team, and I'm confronting challenges with empathy and clarity. I can learn from anyone, I lead by example, and I'm only as good as my last deliverable"_
