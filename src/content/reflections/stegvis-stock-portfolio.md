When I started investing, I paid for a service called Sigma Stocks for about a year. It built a risk-profiled target portfolio, twenty to thirty stocks, and told you exactly what to buy next with each contribution. When you can only invest two or three thousand kronor a month, building real diversification from nothing takes a long time if you're picking stocks yourself, so having something tell you the single best next purchase was genuinely useful.

Then it stopped being useful, not because it got worse, but because I didn't need it anymore. Once I had a substantial, diversified portfolio, I wasn't building anything new, I was just adding to what I already held. Sigma Stocks is built for more than that, ongoing portfolio management, analysis, decisions about when to sell, but I wasn't using any of it. The buildup phase was the part that mattered to me, and it had already done its job.

That's what I kept when I built [Stegvis](https://odectra.github.io/Portfoljbygge/). It's aimed specifically at people who don't yet have the fifty or hundred thousand kronor it takes to buy into a diversified portfolio all at once, and it doesn't try to be anything past that. It picks what to buy next using a simpler set of signals than Sigma Stocks, momentum, growth, cash flow, and I've left the exact logic somewhat open. If you're curious how it decides, you can read up on the terms yourself or look at the code.

I built it the same way I'd approached the [financial business case tool](/reflections/financial-business-case-tool), starting from a real problem I'd already lived through rather than a hypothetical one. It's not financial advice, and it's not meant to replace a portfolio management tool once you've outgrown the buildup phase, the same way Sigma Stocks eventually stopped being what I needed. It's meant to get you started.
